# SPICED Capture Rubric (0 to 3 per dimension)

SPICED is Winning by Design's diagnostic framework (Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision). This rubric is an original operationalization of the 0 to 3 capture idea for transcript scoring. It measures what the CALL captured, in the CUSTOMER's words. It does not measure rep charm, talk ratio, or deal size.

Universal gates that apply to every dimension:

- Score 0 whenever there is no verbatim transcript evidence. No exceptions.
- Levels 2 and 3 require evidence spoken by the customer. Seller statements, even accurate ones, cap the score at 1 (and only when the customer explicitly confirmed them on the call).
- When evidence sits between two levels, assign the lower level.
- Contradictory evidence scores on the weaker reading and gets flagged.

## Situation

What it covers: the customer's current world. Tools in use, team shape and size, volumes, workflow, and whether this account fits the seller's target profile.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not surfaced. No factual picture of the customer's environment was established on the call. |
| 1 | Mentioned but shallow. Generic facts only, the kind already visible on the company website (industry, rough size). Nothing a competitor's rep would not also know. |
| 2 | Explored with specifics. The customer named concrete elements of their environment: which tools, which teams, how many people, how the current workflow runs. |
| 3 | Confirmed with numbers and fit context. The customer stated or confirmed the quantified picture (headcount, volumes, stack) AND enough of it was verified back on the call to judge target fit. A level 3 Situation could brief a colleague who has never seen the account. |

## Pain

What it covers: the acute problem in the customer's own words. Pain is usually emotional language about a process that hurts, a workflow gap, a recurring frustration.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not surfaced. No problem was named, or the call stayed on features and pleasantries. |
| 1 | Mentioned but shallow. A problem was named once without depth, or the rep proposed a pain and the customer merely agreed. Label-level pain ("reporting is a mess") with no example. |
| 2 | Explored with specifics. The customer described the problem in their own words with at least one concrete instance: what happens, who is affected, when it last occurred, what they have tried. |
| 3 | Quantified or confirmed at the source. The customer sized the pain (frequency, hours, money, missed targets) or clearly distinguished the source problem from its symptoms, and confirmed a summary of it on the call. |

Scoring note: pain stated only by the seller ("most teams in your position struggle with X") is not customer pain, whatever the head-nodding.

## Impact

What it covers: the business outcome at stake if the pain is solved. Every real impact lands in one of three buckets: increase revenue, decrease cost, improve customer experience.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not surfaced. Nothing on the call connects the pain to a business outcome. |
| 1 | Mentioned but shallow. A vague benefit was voiced ("this would save us time", "it would help the team") with no bucket, no mechanism, no size. |
| 2 | Explored with specifics. The customer tied the pain to a named business outcome (revenue, cost, or customer experience) and explained the mechanism: what improves, for whom, through what chain. |
| 3 | Quantified and confirmed by the customer. The customer put numbers on it (amount, percentage, hours at a known cost, deals at a known value) or accepted a quantified statement in their own words. The impact could go in a business case with the customer named as its source. |

Scoring note: ROI math the seller performed alone is level 1 at best. Impact only forecasts when the customer owns the number.

## Critical Event

What it covers: the customer's real deadline. The date by which the problem must be solved, and the consequences of missing it. The seller's quarter end is never a critical event.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not surfaced. No timeline discussed, or the customer is openly "just exploring". |
| 1 | Mentioned but shallow. A vague timeframe ("sometime next year", "this is a priority for leadership") with no date and no stated consequence. |
| 2 | Explored with specifics. A specific date or named initiative with a target period exists, but the consequences of missing it were not tested on the call. A date without a tested downside is still soft. |
| 3 | Quantified and confirmed. The customer stated the date AND what happens if it slips (penalty, lost launch, contract expiry, compliance exposure), typically because someone asked the test question: "what happens if you miss that date?" |

Scoring note: the difference between 2 and 3 is exactly one question. If the transcript shows a date but no slip-test, score 2 and put the slip-test at the top of the follow-ups.

## Decision

What it covers: two distinct halves. Process: who is involved, in what order, who signs, and which functions (legal, security, procurement) enter the path. Criteria: what the customer evaluates against and how alternatives rank.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not surfaced. No view of how this customer buys. |
| 1 | Mentioned but shallow. Only the current contact's personal opinion of the process, or a single name ("my VP would need to sign off") with no steps, no committee, no criteria. |
| 2 | Explored with specifics. The customer laid out one half concretely: either the process (named roles, steps, approval path) or the criteria (what matters, must-haves vs nice-to-haves). The other half is still dark or vague. |
| 3 | Confirmed on both halves. The customer described process AND criteria, including signature authority and any legal, security, or procurement steps, and how the seller ranks against the alternatives on those criteria. |

Scoring note: most B2B decisions are made by a committee, not a person. A single-contact view of a committee decision caps at 1 until the committee shape comes from the customer.

## Applying the rubric

1. Collect evidence first, score second. Never pick a score and then hunt for a quote to justify it.
2. Score each dimension independently. A brilliant Pain capture buys nothing for Impact.
3. Record the level-blocking gap for every score below 3: the specific missing element (no number, no date, no slip-test, no committee) that the next call must capture. These gaps feed the follow-up questions directly.
