# The Cannonball Methodology (Core Concepts)

**Attribution:** the concepts on this page (pain-based segmentation, the message-is-the-list principle, PQS and PVP message formats, situational targeting) originate with Jordan Crawford (Blueprint GTM). They are restated here in this skill's own words. All examples on this page are fictional and illustrative.

---

## Core Philosophy

You cannot personalize your way out of a targeting problem. Dramatically better personalization on top of a bad list does not improve conversion. The problem is rarely the message: it is the list. When you know WHO to target and WHY they need you right now, the message writes itself.

**You are not the main character.** Nobody wakes up thinking about your product, your features, or your category. They wake up in a situation. Messaging is the art of describing someone's situation so precisely that replying feels easier than ignoring you.

---

## The Cannonball Framework

1. **Segment identification:** find situations, not personas
2. **Data sources:** use AI to discover HOW to find the segment
3. **Message creation:** PQS and PVP formats
4. **Channel reformatting:** adapt per channel
5. **Scale and repeat:** scale by segment, not by volume

---

## The Three-Question Customer Discovery

1. **Surface level:** "Can you use our product?" (features, compatibility)
2. **Functional level:** "Why do you need this?" (use cases they already know)
3. **Situational level:** "What changed in your organization that made you need this NOW? Why did you decide to buy out of all the other options?"

The third question is the one that matters, and it only comes out once you build rapport. It is rarely on any call transcript.

## Customer Secrets

Most outbound is generic because the sender does not know anything true about the recipient. You need their secrets:

- **What seemed like a good idea but was actually a bad idea.** The initiative that backfired, the vendor that underdelivered, the bet that did not pay off.
- **What they know because of where they are.** The insight that only comes from living inside their role, industry, or situation.
- **How they got to where they are.** What broke, what changed, what shifted that created the current problem.
- **The specific situation that made the "old way" stop working.** The trigger event that turned a tolerable annoyance into an urgent problem.
- **How they would cold email themselves about your thing.** The language, the framing, the proof point that would actually make them reply.

Treat outbound like research: every email is an experiment, every reply is data.

## Segment Identification

Find two actual customers:
- **Low-value customer:** paying X, getting slightly less than X in value
- **High-value customer:** paying X, getting 10X+ in value

Ask: **"What is it about them?"** Identify situational differences, not demographic ones.

## PQS: Pain-Qualified Segment

**Purpose:** re-describe their situation with such precision they think "how did you know?" A PQS is a group of potential customers unified by a pain point, not by demographic or firmographic criteria. Focus on the intensity and specificity of the issue being experienced.

**Structure: Situation, then Insight, then Inquisition**

1. **Situation** (line 1): name the exact condition they are in. Mirror their reality with specific data: tools, dates, numbers, permit IDs, violation records.
2. **Insight** (line 2): reveal something they do not know or have not connected. Not "thought leadership": a take that signals you have seen this exact movie before.
3. **Inquisition** (line 3): ask for truth, not time. "Am I close?" / "Does that resonate?" / "You feeling the same?" The goal is a reply, not a meeting. An Inquisition asks for truth. A CTA asks for time.

**Key rule:** say NOTHING about your product. Only re-describe their situation. No "We are a leading provider..." anywhere.

**Fictional illustration (software vulnerability angle):**

```
Subject: Patched yet?

You're running [FileTransferTool], right? Did you already patch [CVE-XXXX-NNNNN]?

[FileTransferTool] is maintained by a single developer. Five disclosed vulnerabilities in two years.

The last team we spoke to in this exact spot said they felt trapped before they moved. You feeling the same?
```

**Fictional illustration (public permit angle):**

```
Subject: Day 364

Your [City] site (permit #NNNNNNN), filed [date], is scheduled for a three-year review queue.

In your state, accredited private reviewers can approve the same permit class. One a mile from your site cleared in 48 hours.

A third of your open permits qualify. Who's tracking those?
```

## PVP: Permissionless Value Proposition

**Purpose:** a message so valuable prospects would pay to receive it, even if they never buy your product. The email is not "please." It is "I already did the work." You transfer the value IN the email itself.

**Structure:**

1. **Value delivery** (line 1): lead with the work you already did. Specific data, analysis, or intelligence about THEIR business. Not "Have you seen..." but "I already found/built/analyzed..."
2. **Impact** (line 2): connect the data to their specific situation. Make the implication concrete and quantified.
3. **Offer** (line 3): offer the additional deliverable or ask who should receive it. The ask is lightweight because you have already proven value.

**Requirements:**
- The value is IN the message, not promised for later
- The recipient can act on it today without buying anything
- No product mention needed
- "I already did the work" energy, not "want me to do the work?"

**Fictional illustration (idle-asset angle):**

```
Subject: Your [crane model] idle?

Your [crane model] doesn't appear to be in use: no active transport permit on file this quarter.

[NearbyCo] just filed a building permit for a project that typically needs that crane class for 3-6 days. That's a mid-five-figure rental window.

Want an intro?
```

## The Messaging Principles

### You are not the star
Nobody cares about your solution, your features, or your "benefits." They care about the problem they are living in and the daily friction it creates. When you lead with your solution, you are admitting: "I don't understand your problem. I only understand my product."

### Personalize to conditions, not people
"I saw you went to [university]" is not personalization. "We've seen this exact constraint in teams in your situation, and it creates this predictable failure mode" is personalization. Problem before person.

### The anti-patterns (what to stop doing)

1. **Don't talk about you.**
2. **Don't talk about your solution.** No features, no benefits. Only their problem.
3. **Don't lead with case studies.** Social proof is not brands they know; it is peers in their same situation.
4. **Don't assume they believe you.** Name the options. Frame the tradeoffs. Trust is earned by showing you understand alternatives exist.
5. **Don't write long subject lines.** A sharp label for the situation. No brand. No fluff. Never "Quick question."
6. **Don't personalize to the person.** You risk doing more harm than good. Target conditions, not people.
7. **Don't send more than 3 lines.** You are earning a reply, not a sale. Situation, Insight, Inquisition.
8. **Don't spend forever on the message.** Templates beat bespoke. The more time you spend on individual messages, the worse your targeting is.
9. **The list is the message.** Write the perfect message first. Then build the list that makes that message true. Do it backwards.

### Inquisition vs CTA
A CTA asks for time: "Would you be open to a 15-minute call?"
An Inquisition asks for truth: "Am I close, or is it different on your side?"

Replies are the win condition. Not meetings. Not demos. Replies.

### Templates beat bespoke
If you need paragraphs, you do not understand the situation. If you need personalization, your targeting is bad. If you need a call-to-action, your insight is weak.

### Write the message backwards
Write the perfect message as if you had access to all the data in the universe. Then build the list that makes that message true. If your list is just titles and company size, your best email is basically: "Hey, saw you're a VP at a big company." That is not relevance. That is a label.

## AI's Real Role

Use AI for:
- Finding data sources ("How do I find companies that...")
- Writing code to structure data
- Building automations

NOT for:
- Writing customer-facing messages
- Generating "personalized" content
- Inferring situational relevance from demographics

The most valuable AI output in this system is not about your customer. It is the output that helps you find the data showing your customers are in a situation, and structure that data with code.

## Core Principles

### The message IS the list
- If targeting is precise, the message is automatically relevant
- The goal: 100% of the people who receive the message are actually in the situation it describes

### You can't filter your way to a good audience
- Do not start with broad lists and filter down
- Start with the precise segment first
- Build the list from the situation, not vice versa

### Simplicity from precision
- The most effective automations are 3-4 steps
- Complexity exists because targeting was not precise enough
- The complexity does not disappear; it shifts from execution to research

---

## What NOT to Do

- Write persona-level messages
- Use "companies like yours"
- Say "research shows"
- Include any product positioning
- Add explanations of who you are ("We are a leading provider...")
- Use hedge words (might, could, possibly)
- Try to personalize your way out of a targeting problem
- Filter broad lists hoping to find good prospects
- Write long subject lines, or use "Quick question" as a subject line
- Include a CTA that asks for time instead of truth
- Lead with case studies or customer logos
- Send more than 3 lines
- Spend more time on the message than on the list
- **Reference deadlines without checking the current date.** A passed deadline changes the entire play from "upcoming pressure" to "post-deadline emergency"

## The ICP Illusion

"Accounts between $10M and $500M in revenue, with 100 to 1,000 employees, in industries like internet and technology" is what many teams call an ICP. It could describe almost anyone.

The fix: define segments by SITUATIONS that predict purchase, not demographics.

---

## Commoditized vs Intelligence-Driven Signals

**Commoditized (avoid):**
- Funding announcements
- Job changes
- Basic website visits
- "I saw you raised money" (translation: "I like money")

**Intelligence-driven (use):**
- Specific technology usage indicating a situation
- Regulatory changes affecting their specific stack
- Contract expiration data
- Vulnerability exposure data

## Manufacture vs Evaluate

Three ideas that sharpen the whole engine:

**1. Point AI at the records, not the email.** Everyone bought the same AI, so everyone sends the same email. Average is free now; nobody reads the floor. The edge is what you know about the buyer's situation that they cannot see, and it lives in public records no human can read at scale (safety inspections, court dockets, regulator enforcement lists, breach portals, permit systems). AI's job is to read thousands of records and pull the three that matter, with the exact number, date, and dollar figure. When you know exactly who is in pain and can prove it with a record, the words barely matter, but you still have to write them.

**2. Show the math behind every number.** A figure without a retrievable receipt is a fabrication. Every number in a message should trace to a record the buyer could look up. The receipt is the product, not decoration.

**3. Do not believe the machine's copy.** The engine nails the targeting (segment, record, proof) and then drafts copy that reads like a robot reading the database back: specific, competent, lifeless. The draft is a starting point for the ANGLE, never final copy. The last mile is human: take the angle to a real customer who lives in that pain, confirm it lands, then write the note yourself. The machine manufactures the playbooks. You still evaluate them and write the message.

A useful quick-mode target shape: one company URL in, 2 pain-qualified plus 2 permissionless-gift messages out, each with the math behind every number, scored against the internal gates, with confidence graded. The full 9-13 play playbook is the larger deliverable.
