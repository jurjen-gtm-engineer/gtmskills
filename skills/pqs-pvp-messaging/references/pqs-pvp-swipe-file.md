# PQS / PVP Swipe File: annotated examples

Every example here is invented to teach the pattern. The companies, people, addresses and numbers are made up. Study the move, not the words, and mine your own market for the specifics. For Jordan Crawford's original published examples, read his Cannonball newsletter and [Blueprint GTM](https://blueprintgtm.com).

---

## PART 1: PVP examples

The data is the message: concrete to the individual prospect, useful on its own, no product mentioned.

### 1.1 Equipment rental (idle asset next to live demand)
> "Is your 400-tonne crawler crane sitting idle? There is no transit permit on file for it since March. A contractor filed a permit last week to place wind turbines two kilometres from your yard. A lift like that usually rents for a five-figure sum. Want the name of the site lead?"

**Why it is a real PVP:**
- **Data cocktail:** a transit permit registry (no permit means the crane is not moving) and local construction permit filings (a job next door). Two public sources stitched.
- **Concrete:** the asset, the distance, the date, the size of the opportunity.
- **Quantifies the opportunity** instead of naming a pain. It hands them money, not a problem.
- **Useful on its own:** even if they never reply, they now know about a job next door. The ask is a gift, not a demo.

### 1.2 Local services (price benchmark for their postcode)
> "Do you charge 139 a month for pool care? Across the pool companies we see in your region, the going flat rate including chemicals in your postcode is 169, and the range runs from 118 to 184. You may be pricing under the market. Want to see how we know?"

**Why:** the asymmetry comes from aggregated data narrowed to their own postcode. The insight is a number they cannot compute themselves. The ask is low friction and driven by curiosity.

### 1.3 Trades (a lead handed over)
> "The house at [address] sold on 22 October with a 2,000 flooring credit in the offer. The new owner needs flooring work. The listing agent was [name], [public office phone]."

**Why:** a pure gift. It is a lead with an address, a date, an amount and a public contact. There is no ask in the body; the value is complete, so the reply comes by itself. Only use contact details that are published for business use.

### 1.4 More PVP cocktails (pattern table)
| Vertical | Data cocktail | The insight (quantified) |
|---|---|---|
| Dental | public health data by city, plus location | a city-level case for one specific service line |
| Construction | material price indices | "Concrete is up 22% in 45 days in your county; here are three other suppliers" |
| Restaurants | licence auction records | "A licence like this is at auction below market, two streets from you" |
| Any platform business | your own platform's history across customers | "The launches that worked all set [variable] near [threshold]. You are about to launch one." |

### 1.5 The "predict the future" PVP (full shape)
> "We have watched 12 product launches like yours in the last 12 months. The 4 that took off all set the sign-up bonus within 10% of 8,000. The 8 that stalled were below 5,000 or above 12,000. You are about to launch one. I do not know your numbers, but here is our spread. Want me to send the version for your second product line?"

**Why this is the template to learn:** a real count, a measurable threshold, an honest disclaimer ("I do not know your numbers") that keeps it from being creepy, the buyer runs the math against their own plan, and the ask offers more value, not a meeting.

## PART 2: The 4 delivery shapes (when a blunt PVP needs softening)

### 2.1 Praise for the past
> "Good that you moved off manual reconciliation. Nine finance teams your size that stayed on it missed their audit date last year."

The same insight as a warning, but pointed at the past, so the buyer is the hero. No defense needed.

### 2.2 Reply to their newsletter
> "You sent this on Tuesday. The subject line is nine words; in our data, short ones get opened more. Try this: [rewrite]. Want me to look at the last six the same way?"

Sent as a reply to their own newsletter, with the marketing lead added by hand. The gift is already in their inbox.

### 2.3 Name three
> "Three of your customers, [X], [Y] and [Z], each [one specific thing about each that they did not know]. Worth a look."

Three forces the thought "they have data". The buyer checks it themselves. You do not have to be complete, you have to be specific and right.

### 2.4 Not sure whose this is
> "Not sure if this belongs to [name] or [name]. Your sign-up form still accepts personal email addresses, and your pricing page says business only. Thought you should know. There is more if useful."

No ask, so nothing to push back on. A light tone carries the call to action.

## PART 3: PQS examples

A PQS mirrors the situation, names a bite they had not connected, and asks a low-friction question. No product. Recognition is the give.

### 3.1 Payments (illumination question)
> Subject: {{first_name}}, chargebacks
>
> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> Looks like {{company_name}} takes payments through [terminal provider].
>
> When a chargeback comes in, does someone still pull the evidence together, or has the team found it is not worth the time?
>
> A bakery chain about your size ties video to each sale and wins most of them back without digging.
>
> Worth a look?
>
> Either way, 17 years of keeping staff and regulars happy is no small thing.

**Why:** situation, then a question that exposes a process people quietly gave up on (the bite), then "without" (no digging), a detached ask and a specific, honest compliment. The question can be answered either way.

### 3.2 Secure file transfer (data-backed)
> "{{first_name}}, your team appears to use [platform] for secure file transfers.
>
> When a vulnerability is published against that platform, like the one on [date], patching usually takes weeks. For regulated transfers, that is an open compliance gap every day it sits.
>
> Teams in this spot tell us the worst part is not the vulnerability. It is not knowing how long the exposure lasts.
>
> Does that match what you see?"

**Why:** situation (the tool), why (the patch window), bite (the uncertainty they feel but cannot name), then a confirm question. The bite is the unnamed worry, not the obvious bug.

### 3.3 ERP migration (the unbudgeted rebuild)
> "{{first_name}}, I saw you are moving to a new ERP for two countries while the customised old system still runs the core.
>
> The new data model means part of your integrations do not move over, they have to be rebuilt. Most implementation partners scope for migrating, not rebuilding. That backlog is rarely on the program's own budget.
>
> Is that something you are in the middle of right now?"

**Why:** signal (a specific migration), why (new data model), bite (the rebuild nobody budgeted), then a soft confirm. A second email can carry proof: a similar company by type and stage with one concrete outcome, never named.

### 3.4 New building (a decision that locks in for years)
> "I saw {{company_name}} is opening a new site in {{city}}, with handover around {{year}}.
>
> The locking plan is fixed during construction. If it goes mechanical, that choice stands for twenty years. How will you show, per shift, who had access where?"

**Why:** signal (new site, build phase), why (the plan is locked during construction), then a real question about proof of access. It never says "you must comply now".

### 3.5 PQS skeleton (any language)
> Subject: {{first_name}}, [2-word topic]
>
> {{first_name}}, [SITUATION: the signal as their reality, one sentence].
>
> [THE WHY: the mechanism] [THE BITE: the unbudgeted second-order problem].
>
> [CONFIRM: one low-friction question, five words to answer, not a meeting].

## PART 4: Side-by-side: same signal, PQS vs PVP

Signal: *a company is running a tech with a newly published CVE.*

**PQS (Lane C, you only know the situation):**
> "{{first_name}}, looks like {{company_name}} runs [platform] for secure transfers. When a CVE lands, patching usually takes weeks, and for regulated data, that's an open window the whole time. The hard part teams tell us about isn't the bug, it's not knowing how long they're exposed. Does that match your world?"

**PVP (Lane A, you stitched data they don't track):**
> "{{first_name}}, [platform] picked up CVE-2026-XXXX on [date] (CVSS 9.1). Your [subdomain] is still on the affected build per its response headers as of [date]. Across the 40+ HIPAA-regulated orgs we track on this platform, median patch time after a KEV listing was 19 days, and the regulator's last three settlements for this gap were in the tens of thousands per breach. Here's the one-page exposure read for your domain. Want the patch-priority version?"

**The difference:** the PQS *describes a situation they recognize*. The PVP *tells them something specific about their own systems they didn't know* (their subdomain's actual build), benchmarks it against a tracked cohort (Data Key), times it (Leading indicator), and prices the risk (Trailing indicator). One earns "how did they know?"; the other earns "I need to forward this to security."

## PART 5: Failure gallery (what to never ship)

| ❌ Bad | Why it dies | ✅ Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "I help [industry] companies streamline [thing]. Quick demo?" | Generic, product-first, zero asymmetry | Mine a real signal; mirror it (PQS) or stitch data (PVP) |
| "I saw you're hiring a senior engineer, we save 40%." | Differentiation trap; every agency sends this | Tell them something about the *role/company* nobody else can |
| "Food costs are rising, want to cut them?" (Captain Obvious) | Names an obvious pain, no asymmetry | "Concrete +22% in your county in 45 days, 3 alt suppliers" |
| "Based on our analysis you're underperforming peers." | Benchmark, not prediction; vague | Predict their future with a count + threshold |
| A "PVP" that's a clever line + "book 30 min" | Fake PVP, not independently useful | Make the body useful with no reply, or downgrade to PQS |
| Long email showing all the data work | Data-theater, no empathy | Read it aloud to a real customer; cut to one human helping another |
